# Code of Conduct

This is a small project with one maintainer and a teaching purpose. The rules
are short because they can be.

## What is expected

Assume the other person is trying to learn something or build something, and
answer accordingly. Most people who turn up here are learning PLC programming,
which means a lot of questions will look basic to somebody who already knows the
answer. That is the point of the project. Treating those questions as beneath a
reply is the one thing most likely to make this space useless.

Beyond that, the ordinary things: be civil about disagreements, criticise the
work rather than the person, and accept that the maintainer may say no to
something you spent time on.

## What is not acceptable

* Harassment, personal attacks, or demeaning comments — including about somebody's
  experience level, the tools they are stuck with, the vendor they are forced to
  use, or their English.
* Discrimination or slurs of any kind: race, nationality, religion, gender,
  gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, appearance.
* Sexual language or imagery, and unwanted sexual attention.
* Publishing somebody's private information without their permission.
* Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments to wear people down,
  brigading an issue, or using the tracker as a soapbox.

## Where this applies

Every space the project occupies — issues, pull requests, commit messages, code
comments, discussions — and any place where somebody is plainly representing the
project.

## Reporting

**If it is not sensitive**, open an issue and say what happened.

**If it is sensitive**, or it concerns the maintainer, report it privately to the
repository owner, [@malgaroshy-maker](https://github.com/malgaroshy-maker), via
GitHub. Do not put it in a public issue first.

Reports will be read. They will not necessarily be answered quickly — this is one
person working alone, and pretending otherwise would be the first dishonest thing
in this repository.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit or delete comments, close issues and pull requests, or
block an account, and does not owe a public explanation for doing so in a case
involving somebody's private report.

For a fuller and more carefully argued document than this one, the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/)
is the standard text most projects adopt. This file is a plain-language statement
of the same expectations, written to match the rest of the documentation here
rather than to replace that work.
